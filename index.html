Substitute shift replacement

Can't make your shift?

Every shift is done in full, no matter who shows up. An uncovered absence becomes overtime for your teammates.

What kind of absence?

Planned

  1. Get Kitchen Manager approval at least 1 week ahead. Ask before you arrange anything. A swap the Kitchen Manager hasn't approved doesn't count.
  2. Call your backups to arrange a substitute.
  3. Your substitute confirms with the Kitchen Manager. They tell the Kitchen Manager themselves, so there's no doubt they said yes.
  4. Covered

Sick or unexpected

  1. Act as soon as you feel unwell, even if you're unsure. Don't wait for the shift. The earlier you start, the easier it is to find someone.
  2. Email the Kitchen Manager and Work Study Manager.
  3. Phone your backups. Call, don't just text.
  4. Did someone say yes within 30 minutes?
    YesYour substitute confirms with the Kitchen Manager.
    NoCall the Kitchen Manager directly.
  5. Covered
Afterward

Make up your missed hours. If you swapped, do your substitute's duty as agreed.

It's not covered until someone says yes.

Cleanup policies

What every shift does, in order, and the rules that keep the kitchen safe for the next people who use it.

1

Arriving

  • Be there at the start of the shift, ready to work.
  • Tie long hair back, put on an apron and wash your hands before touching anything.
  • Wear closed-toe, non-slip shoes. Floors get wet.
  • Phones go in your bag or pocket, not on the counters.
  • Check the board for anything the Kitchen Manager has left for today's shift.
2

The cleanup, in order

  1. Clear and scrape. Bring in every dish, scrape food into compost, and stack by type.
  2. Put food away. See Food safety before anything goes in the fridge.
  3. Dishes. Wash, rinse, sanitize, then air-dry on the racks. No towel-drying.
  4. Pots and pans. Soak the hard ones first and come back to them.
  5. Counters and tables. Wipe down, then sanitize, including the edges and the undersides of lips.
  6. Stove and equipment. Let it cool first. Wipe the knobs and the fronts.
  7. Floors. Sweep first, then mop, working toward the door. Put out the wet-floor sign.
  8. Trash, recycling and compost. Take each one out, rinse the bin if it needs it, and put a new liner in.
3

Food safety

  • Leftovers go in a covered container, labelled with what it is and today's date.
  • Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of serving. Shallow containers cool fastest.
  • Nothing is stored on the floor, and raw ingredients go below ready-to-eat food.
  • Keep allergen-free dishes and utensils separate. If you're not sure, ask.
  • If something looks or smells off, don't taste it: tell the Kitchen Manager.
4

Chemicals and safety

  • Never mix cleaning products, bleach and ammonia above all.
  • Use only labelled spray bottles. If a bottle has no label, don't use it.
  • Wear gloves for sanitizer and degreaser, and follow the dilution on the label.
  • The safety data sheets are kept by the chemical shelf. Know where they are.
  • Report any cut, burn, spill or breakage to the Kitchen Manager the same day, however small.
5

Before you leave

  • Walk the kitchen once with your crew: sinks empty, counters dry, floors done.
  • Dish towels and aprons go in the laundry bin.
  • Stove, oven and burners off. Fridge and freezer doors shut tight.
  • Sign the shift sheet. The shift isn't over until it's signed.
  • Lights off and the door locked if you're the last ones out.
6

Attendance

  • If you can't make a shift, follow the call-out steps. It isn't covered until someone says yes.
  • Arriving more than 10 minutes late counts as a late arrival. Three in a term means a talk with the Work Study Manager.
  • Make up missed hours within two weeks, at a time the Kitchen Manager agrees to.
  • If you swapped, do your substitute's shift as agreed. A swap is a promise.
  • An uncovered absence is reported to the Work Study Manager.
